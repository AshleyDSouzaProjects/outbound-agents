# 20 — India-Specific Context

Principles imported from US investors need adjustment. In India, **governance risk — not
valuation risk — is the dominant cause of permanent capital impairment.** A US-calibrated
quality screen applied unmodified to Indian mid-caps will walk directly into frauds.

---

## 1. Promoter dominance

Most Indian listed companies have a controlling "promoter" family holding 40–75%. Consequences:

- Minority shareholders are **passive passengers**. There is no activist path, no proxy fight
  that wins, no board you can influence.
- The controlling shareholder's incentives may diverge sharply from yours, and they have the
  votes to act on that divergence.
- Succession is a genuine, under-priced risk. Second- and third-generation transitions destroy
  a great deal of value.

**Therefore:** promoter quality assessment is not optional colour, it is a core analytical
input, weighted comparably to business quality.

## 2. Promoter pledging — a near-absolute veto

Promoters pledging shares to raise personal debt is the highest-signal red flag in Indian
markets. The mechanism is vicious and reflexive: a price fall triggers a margin call, which
forces pledged-share sales, which drives the price down further.

- Pledge > 25% of promoter holding → **hard reject.**
- Pledge rising over consecutive quarters → **hard reject**, regardless of level.
- Any pledge at all → mandatory investigation of *why* the promoter needs personal liquidity.

Disclosed quarterly in the shareholding pattern. There is no excuse for missing it.

## 3. Related-party transactions — the primary expropriation mechanism

**This is how minority shareholders are actually harmed in India** — far more often than by
outright fraud. Value leaves the listed entity through:

- Sales to, or purchases from, promoter-owned unlisted entities at non-market prices.
- Royalty or brand-licensing fees paid to a promoter entity.
- Loans and advances to subsidiaries or associates that are never repaid.
- Corporate guarantees given for group companies.
- Rent paid to promoter-owned property at inflated rates.

**Therefore:** RPT analysis is a Tier-2 rejection filter. Track RPT as a percentage of revenue
and of PAT, and the *trend*. Rising RPT intensity is a reject. Use the MCA21/RoC filings of the
**counterparty** — the unlisted entity's own accounts often reveal what the listed entity's
disclosure obscures. This is precisely the sort of cross-entity work agents make feasible.

## 4. Auditor signals — the best leading indicator available

In order of severity:

1. **Auditor resignation mid-term** — treat as a five-alarm fire. Near-always precedes trouble.
2. **Auditor change without clear explanation** — severe.
3. **Qualified opinion or emphasis of matter** — investigate fully.
4. **Adverse CARO observations** — investigate.
5. Small or unknown audit firm for a large company — structural concern.

Several major Indian collapses were preceded by auditor exits *quarters before* the equity
market reacted. The information was public and sitting in exchange filings.

**Therefore:** auditor-event monitoring is a continuous Tier-2 process with hard-reject authority.

## 5. Group structures and holdcos

Indian conglomerates run opaque webs of cross-holdings. Watch for circular shareholdings,
holdcos trading at persistent 50–70% NAV discounts (usually *justified* by governance, not a
bargain), and cash trapped at subsidiary level.

**Therefore:** analyse the *consolidated* entity, and separately check what the standalone-vs-
consolidated gap conceals. A large and growing gap is a red flag by itself.

## 6. Accounting-specific watch items

- **Ind AS transition (~FY16–17)** breaks naive 10-year series. Handle the discontinuity
  explicitly or restrict the comparison window.
- **Capitalisation of interest and development costs** — common margin-flattering device.
- **Contingent liabilities** — often larger than net worth; frequently ignored. Read the note.
- **Tax disputes** — endemic; assess which are genuinely likely to crystallise.
- **Receivable days drift** in infrastructure, capital goods and government-facing businesses —
  the classic revenue-recognition tell.
- **Inventory build** in consumer and pharma without matching revenue growth — channel stuffing.
- **Subsidiary losses** netted against a healthy standalone parent.

## 7. Sector-specific cautions

- **Financials (banks, NBFCs)** — asset quality is unknowable from outside with any confidence.
  Reported NPAs are a lagging, management-influenced number. **Default to out-of-circle**
  (see `11-buffett-munger.md` on circle of competence) unless there is a specific, defensible
  reason to make an exception.
- **Infrastructure / EPC** — percentage-of-completion accounting; receivables; government
  counterparty risk. Structurally hostile to outside analysis.
- **Real estate** — inventory valuation, land-bank accounting, opaque JV structures.
- **Pharma** — USFDA action risk is binary, unforecastable, and can permanently impair value.
- **PSUs** — the government's objectives are not shareholder value; treat capital allocation
  as exogenous and unpredictable.
- **Commodities** — no pricing power by construction; fails the moat test absent a genuine,
  structural cost advantage.

## 8. Market structure realities

- **Liquidity** — much of the Nifty 500 tail is thin. A position we cannot exit in a reasonable
  number of days is a position we should not size meaningfully.
- **Small/mid-cap drawdowns of 50–60% are normal**, not exceptional. Position sizing must
  assume them rather than treat them as tail events.
- **Retail flows via SIPs** have structurally supported mid-cap valuations since ~2016. This is
  a *flow* phenomenon, not a fundamental one, and it can reverse.
- **Disclosure quality varies enormously** across the 500. Absence of disclosure is itself
  information, and usually negative.

## 9. The validation set

Any rejection filter must be tested against these, on **point-in-time data only** (see
`../CLAUDE.md` §1). If the filters do not reject these before their collapse, they are worthless:

| Company | Failure mode |
|---|---|
| Satyam (2009) | Fabricated cash; auditor failure |
| Yes Bank (2020) | Concealed asset quality; promoter conduct |
| DHFL (2019) | Related-party lending; diversion |
| IL&FS (2018) | Group opacity; leverage; rating failure |
| Manpasand Beverages (2018) | Auditor resignation; revenue fabrication |
| Vakrangee (2018) | Auditor resignation; price manipulation |
| Zee / Essel (2019) | Promoter pledging cascade |
| Coffee Day (2019) | Promoter debt; RPT diversion |
| Gitanjali Gems (2018) | Outright fraud; RPT |

Note the recurring mechanisms: **auditor exits, promoter pledging, related-party diversion.**
Three signals, all disclosed in public filings, all available in advance. The Tier-2 filters
exist to catch exactly these.

---

## Encoded rules

1. Promoter pledge > 25%, or rising → hard reject.
2. Auditor resignation → hard reject pending full investigation.
3. Rising RPT intensity → hard reject.
4. Cash conversion persistently < 80% → hard reject.
5. Financials, real estate and EPC → out-of-circle by default.
6. Growing standalone-vs-consolidated gap → investigate before anything else.
7. Contingent liabilities > net worth → hard reject.
8. Assume 50–60% drawdowns are normal; size accordingly.
